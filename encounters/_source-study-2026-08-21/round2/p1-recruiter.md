# Recruiter screen: Atomize AI "Fact Refinery" explainer

Reviewer: P1 (technical recruiter, AI/data eng screens). Not an engineer — I judge whether this reads as a real system built by someone who'd pass a bar-raiser loop, or a portfolio toy dressed up with jargon.

Page: [local snapshot: eval-snapshot.html.] Reviewed FLOOR and BLUEPRINTS tabs, desktop (1440px) and mobile (390px), with live interaction (toggles, buttons, a query run, a 24h simulation, node inspection, tab switches).

---

## 1. Ten seconds in — first read

First things visible: "THE FACT REFINERY" logo top-left, then the giant headline "THE ATOMIZE AI PLANT REFINES RAW TEXT INTO INTELLIGENT NEWS," and immediately under it: **"This is a demo of the part of our news tool that checks facts before they get used, built so you can see exactly what it keeps and what it throws away, and why."**

That second line is what decided it for me, not the headline. The headline is marketing-flavored ("plant," "refines") and I almost bounced on it — sounds like a metaphor someone fell in love with. But "demo of the part of our news tool" plus "built so you can see exactly what it keeps and what it throws away, and why" told me this is scoped and specific, not a vague AI-startup landing page. I kept reading.

Also decisive, a few lines down: **"Atomize reads the day's AI news and research, then keeps only the facts it can prove with a verbatim quote."** That's a concrete, falsifiable claim, not "AI-powered insights." That's the sentence that made me trust there's an actual person who understands what they built, not a template.

## 2. What is this system actually for, in my own words

It's a fact-checking gate inside an AI news pipeline. Someone built a news/research aggregation product (Atomize AI, feeding rosslabs.ai and other surfaces) that pulls in articles, papers, and threads from five source types, runs them through an LLM extraction step, and only keeps a "fact" if the model can point to the exact quote in the source text that supports it — no quote, no fact. Rejected content gets a specific reason code, not a silent drop. The kept facts go into a Postgres+pgvector graph, and a query system retrieves from that graph to power downstream products (a news feed, a podcast, an admin inbox). This page is a scale-model simulation of that pipeline you can operate — toggle gates, feed articles, run the day — to see the real gate logic without hitting the live system.

I can tell this because the copy is repetitive about it in a good way: three separate sections restate the same "toy has a real production counterpart" idea from different angles, and by section 4 they spell out gate-by-gate what's real code vs. simulated numbers.

## 3. Where I stopped, reread, or almost gave up

- **"IT DRIVES DOWNSTREAM AI SPEND"** next to the Gate Power toggle, with the subtext *"The shipped sample environment sets this flag to false, so treat this switch as a real code path rather than a proven production setting."* I stopped and reread this twice. It's an admission that a production flag currently defaults OFF in the shipped sample and they're telling me not to over-trust it. That's either impressive honesty or a red flag depending on my mood — I landed on impressive, because most people hide that line, they don't put it in bold-adjacent copy on their own demo page.
- **Section 02 headline: "THIS IS THE BEST EVIDENCE ON THE PAGE, AND IT STILL IS NOT SHIPPING."** I stopped hard here. A builder admitting their best evidence isn't in production is not something a portfolio-polisher writes. I reread the panel under it three times because I wanted to know if the numbers backing that claim were real (they cite a specific commit hash and "64 live queries" — see item 4).
- **"daily at NaN:05 UTC" / "daily at NaN:15 UTC" / "daily at NaN:00 UTC"** in the job-select dropdown (desktop, observed via the accessibility tree before I even screenshotted). I stopped on this because it's the one place the "everything here is real" claim visibly cracks — a `NaN` leaking into user-facing copy is a raw JS Date bug, not a simulation choice. I went looking for it on screen and couldn't easily find it rendered (it's inside a `<select>` I couldn't get to open in my browser tooling), but it's sitting in the accessible name of three menu options, which any screen reader user or anyone tabbing through would hit.
- **"Select a job" dropdown.** I tried to operate it three separate times and could not get it to open or select an option. I don't fully trust that this is the site's fault vs. my tooling, but I'm noting it because if I were doing this exercise as a hiring manager on my own laptop, a control that won't respond is a control that won't respond — I don't get to know it's "probably just headless Chrome."
- **The reject-chute reason code tiles** ("quote not grounded," "confidence < 0.5," "off-vocabulary relation," "type constraint failed" etc.) — I stopped and read every one of these because they're specific enough to be believable engineering vocabulary, not filler. This is where I moved from "maybe" to "probably real."

## 4. What built trust, what built doubt

**Trust:**
- Named, falsifiable numbers with provenance, not vibes: "Source: commit 36df9d062, differential run, 64 live queries. 45 of 64 queries retrieve differently, 70.3%. This size is the stated reason the flag stays off." That's an actual engineering decision with an actual reason, not a marketing claim. If a candidate said this in an interview I'd believe they shipped something.
- The BLUEPRINTS tab's confidence-badge legend (MEASURED / REASONED / INFERRED / DEFAULT / BUILT NOT WIRED / NO RECORD) applied consistently to individual claims ("A trigram index was deliberately hidden from Prisma to avoid locking the table" — tagged INFERRED). This is the single strongest signal on the page. It's a self-imposed epistemics system for a demo page. Most candidates don't even do this for their actual documentation.
- The reject-chute reason codes and the "It drives X" labels under every control are specific to a real content pipeline (SHA-256 dedupe keys, SSRF guard, closed vocabulary of 26 relation types, pgvector vs. adjacency list tradeoffs). This isn't generic AI-demo vocabulary; it's the kind of detail you only write down if you've debugged the actual thing.
- Live, working interaction: toggling Gate Power actually changed copy and downstream stats; feeding articles actually populated a graph visualization with growing reject/fact counters in real time; "RUN A QUERY" refused to run and told me why ("needs at least 5 facts, 0 of 5 so far") instead of faking success. A toy would have let the query "succeed" against nothing.
- Section 02's confession that the better system isn't shipped. Nobody writing pure marketing copy volunteers that their own top feature is disabled in prod.

**Doubt:**
- The `NaN:05 UTC` / `NaN:15 UTC` / `NaN:00 UTC` timestamps. If the whole pitch is "everything on this page is either real or honestly labeled synthetic," a raw `NaN` leaking into a schedule label undercuts that pitch specifically, because it looks like nobody read their own dropdown before shipping the demo.
- A non-functional dropdown control (see #3) is a small thing but sits right next to a giant "watch the real cron schedule" claim — the one interactive element I couldn't get to respond was the one meant to prove the schedule is real and specific.
- The "RUN THE DAY" button became "PAUSE THE DAY" on click but the status line still read "Running the day, 24 hours in about 60 seconds" for at least one render after I paused it — a small state-lag, not fatal, but it's the kind of thing that in a real product would eventually confuse someone watching it.
- Everything is explicitly self-labeled "SYNTHETIC RUN, not telemetry" / "synthetic, no external baseline" for the live counters (articles fed, rejects/min, etc.). That's honest, but it also means none of the numbers I watched move — 12 articles fed, 93% graph trust, $0.00 downstream spend — are proof of anything beyond "the simulation's internal math is self-consistent." I want to be clear I'm not crediting the live counters as evidence; I'm crediting the commit-hash-and-differential-test claim in Section 02 as evidence, because that one cites something outside the toy.

## 5. As a recruiter — does this raise or lower my read, and what would I ask

**Raises it, meaningfully.** This is one of the more credible AI-engineering artifacts I've been asked to screen. The reason isn't the visuals (which are fine, if maybe over-produced for a "demo"), it's that the author clearly understands the difference between "I built a thing" and "I can tell you exactly which parts are measured, which are inferred, and which are a coin flip nobody's tuned since." That epistemic discipline — the MEASURED/REASONED/INFERRED/DEFAULT/BUILT NOT WIRED/NO RECORD taxonomy, applied to their own claims, in a demo page — is not something I see from candidates who are performing expertise. People who are performing expertise don't admit their best classifier isn't shipped because the blast radius is too big.

What I'd ask in a screen:
- "Walk me through the 45-of-64-queries number. What was the differential harness, and why did the team decide 70% behavior change was too large to ship, instead of, say, canarying it?" — I want to hear if they can defend that decision live, or if it was someone else's call they're just describing.
- "The Gate Power flag defaults off in your shipped sample. Is that also true in production today, or is that a demo-specific default? If it's true in prod, why is a content-integrity gate off by default?" — this is the one claim on the page that reads as a real risk (money getting spent on ungated content) and I want to know if they know it's a risk.
- "What's actually stored where — I saw 'Postgres + pgvector' called out as deliberately not a graph database. Why that tradeoff, and what would make you revisit it?"
- "There's a NaN showing up in one of your cron schedule labels. Do you know why, and would that same bug exist anywhere in the production scheduler, or is it purely a demo-rendering issue?" — I ask this not to gotcha them, but because their reaction tells me a lot: do they know their own code well enough to answer instantly, or are they surprised?
- "Who is the audience for this page — is this something you'd actually show a customer, or is it internal engineering communication? " — because the tone (self-deprecating, evidence-heavy) reads more like an internal design-review artifact than a sales page, and I want to know if that's intentional.

## 6. Visibly broken / misaligned / overlapping / cut off

- **Desktop (1440px), accessible-name level, not yet confirmed visually rendered:** three cron job entries in the "JUMP TO A JOB BY KEYBOARD" select read "/api/cron/content-backfill, daily at NaN:05 UTC", "/api/cron/embed-backfill, daily at NaN:15 UTC", "/api/cron/detect-releases, daily at NaN:00 UTC" — a JS date-formatting bug leaking `NaN` into user-facing text.
- **Desktop (1440px):** the "Select a job" dropdown would not open or accept a selection under automated interaction across three attempts — worth a manual click-test on a real trackpad before calling it done, since I couldn't confirm whether this is a real defect or a tooling limitation on my end.
- **Mobile (390px):** the header logo renders as "FACT REFINERY" — the word "THE" from the desktop "THE FACT REFINERY" wordmark is not present at mobile width. I could not confirm from the screenshot alone whether this is a deliberate responsive shortening or a clipped/hidden element; worth a look at the actual CSS.
- **Mobile (390px):** the FLOOR/BLUEPRINTS tab buttons measure roughly 112×33px — under the standard 44px mobile touch-target minimum on the vertical axis. Small but real; someone with imprecise motor control will mis-tap between them.
- **Mobile (390px):** the ACQUIRE row's five source-column headers (RSS / Maps / Papers / Talk / Bench) compress into ~65px-wide columns and the "dedupe, SHA-256(title,link,date-bucket), SSRF guard" caption that's fully visible on desktop shows only as "dedupe" on mobile — I couldn't tell from the screenshot whether the rest is truncated/hidden-on-purpose or clipped by a fixed-width container; flagging it as a "look closer" item, not a confirmed bug.
- **State lag, both widths:** after clicking "PAUSE THE DAY," the status line under the dial still read "Running the day, 24 hours in about 60 seconds" in the same render pass — a one-frame inconsistency between button state and status copy.
- Nothing else broke. No overlapping text, no cut-off cards, no console-visible layout collision in anything I actually watched render — the responsive behavior (collapsing the Plant Controls panel to a one-line summary on mobile, adding a sticky MODE/CONTROLS bar at the bottom, stacking the five-stage grid to one column) was consistently well-executed.

## 7. The single change that would most improve it for me

**Fix the NaN and prove the "everything real is labeled real" claim holds under my own poking, not just under a quick skim.** The entire pitch of this page is a trust claim — "operate the gates, watch reason codes attach to real code paths, MEASURED vs INFERRED vs NO RECORD." That's a strong, specific, unusual claim, and it earned my trust fast. But a candidate whose central pitch is "you can verify everything here yourself" loses more credibility per bug than one who never made that claim in the first place — the bar for a self-verifying system is that it survives verification. One `NaN` in a schedule label is small in isolation, but it's exactly the kind of small thing that, if I found it after they told me "this is the real cron schedule," would make me go back and re-check every other number on the page with more suspicion than I started with. Fix that, confirm the job-select dropdown actually opens for a normal user, and this jumps from "a very strong artifact" to "the strongest technical-screen artifact I've seen this cycle."
