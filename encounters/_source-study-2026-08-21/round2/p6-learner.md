# P6 — AI-native learner review of eval-snapshot.html ("The Fact Refinery")

Tested at [local snapshot: eval-snapshot.html] via IBR (session_start/session_action/screenshot/observe/scan). Desktop viewport 1440x900 unless noted; mobile viewport 390x844. Server had to be restarted mid-review (see §6) — noting that up front because it colors how much I trust the rest of the infra.

## 1. What I actually learned (new to me, with the number/mechanism)

- **Entity extraction runs per-KG-block on `openai/gpt-oss-20b` through Groq**, strict JSON schema, temperature 0, low reasoning effort, 4096 max output tokens. The code hard-throws if the configured model isn't in the gpt-oss family because only that family supports the required strict schema mode. I didn't know Groq was in the loop for structured extraction, or that model-family was a hard runtime assertion, not a soft preference.
- **KG blocks are sized at 8000 target tokens, 256 overlap, 10000 hard max — deliberately larger than the 800-token retrieval chunks.** Retrieval wants small precise spans; extraction wants enough surrounding context to ground a quote. Two different chunk sizes for two different jobs, on purpose. I'd never separated those two chunk-size decisions before.
- **Quote-grounding gate: real measured hallucination numbers.** Audit found 17% hallucination rate at n=30. A looser policy shipped first and was re-measured at n=120: hallucination fell to 2.5%, but a new "ungrounded" failure class rose to 13%. Quote grounding then shipped to close that gap. The page states plainly: "Zero rows have been recorded since deploy, so the final rate is not yet re-verified." That's a real number with an admitted expiration date, not a marketing stat.
- **Relationship extraction: closed vocabulary of 26 relation types across two families** (business: develops, acquires, invests_in, partners_with, competes_with, sues; research: authored_by, cites, builds_on, trained_on, evaluated_on, outperforms). Filters run in a fixed order: vocabulary membership → strength ≥0.5 → both endpoint names literally present in the block text → per-relation type constraints. Capital-flow fields (dollar amounts, %) are populated only for invests_in/acquires and non-USD currencies are rejected to null.
- **Index inventory names real HNSW parameters**: `idx_embedding_chunks_hnsw` m=16, ef_construction=64, cosine (the primary RAG path) vs. `article_embeddings_embedding_idx` m=24, ef_construction=100 (raised above defaults for accuracy, different table). I didn't know a single system would carry two different HNSW tuning profiles for different accuracy/recall tradeoffs.
- **A demonstrably better retrieval router is deliberately NOT shipped.** Intent Cascade routing (three-tier classifier) was A/B tested on 64 live production queries against commit `36df9d062`: 28 better, 4 worse, 13 neutral. Net positive. But 45 of 64 queries (70.3%) retrieve *differently* — and that blast radius, not the quality number, is the stated reason the flag stays off. This is a genuinely useful lesson: "net better" and "safe to ship" are different bars, and the page shows the actual math behind that decision instead of just asserting it.
- **The daily cron topology**: 16 Vercel cron routes fire on a schedule through the UTC day; 6 Railway workers stay always-on instead, pulsing only on queue events. A 02:00–04:30 UTC shaded maintenance band on the dial marks cleanup/benchmark-refresh/model-classification clustering. Vercel = clock-driven, Railway = queue-driven — laid out as one dial so the contrast is visual, not just described.
- **The "Maintenance Log" (BLUEPRINTS, section 04) lists 9 places where the repo contradicts itself**, each with a named resolution action. Concrete standouts: two migration systems (Prisma-tracked vs Supabase-tracked) both claim ownership of the same HNSW index on `article_embeddings.embedding`; a spaCy-based direction-check service is deployed and billed on Railway with **zero confirmed callers** ("No document in the repository records a decision not to use it. The absence of a recorded reason is itself the finding."); every "circuit breaker" in the read path is actually a try-catch-with-fallback, no open/half-open state machine exists despite a `circuit_states` table; a multi-hop RAG implementation (`agentic-rag.ts`, `kg-rag.ts`, `grounded-retrieval.ts`) exists with no route calling it. This is dead-code and cost-waste disclosure I've never seen a vendor demo admit to.

## 2. Dead / broken / surprising controls — exhaustive

This is the part that matters most, per instructions, so I'm listing every single one.

- **All 10 reject-reason tiles** (`quality_below_floor`, `known_link_hub`, `page_chrome_signature`, `sponsored_content_quarantine`, `quote not grounded`, `confidence < 0.5`, `strength < 0.5`, `endpoint not in text`, `off-vocabulary relation`, `type constraint failed`) render as `role=button` with a full descriptive accessible name, look identical in styling to the counter tiles above them (which DO open a detail panel), and **do nothing when clicked.** I confirmed this repeatedly, both while auto-feed was running (could plausibly be a race with live re-render) and after I explicitly paused auto-feed so the DOM was static — still not clickable (`not actionable: not resolvable`) in either state. These are the single most inviting-looking dead controls on the page — they sit right next to the ARTICLES FED / FACTS KEPT tiles that genuinely do expand.
- **All 9 counter tiles** (ARTICLES FED, DUPES FOLDED, FACTS KEPT, FACTS REJECTED, REJECTS/MIN, DOWNSTREAM AI SPEND, INTAKE/MIN, QUERIES SERVED, GRAPH TRUST) are also tagged as clickable buttons with rich captions ("synthetic, no external baseline", "% of resolved facts", etc.) but none of them opened anything when clicked — I tried `FACTS KEPT` specifically and got the same "not resolvable" failure.
- **BLUEPRINTS "Stage 1 / Stage 2 / Stage 3 / Stage 4 / Stage 5" nav strip** (top of the BLUEPRINTS tab) is styled exactly like the FLOOR tab's working ACQUIRE/TRANSFORM/STORE/RETRIEVE/SURFACE band-jump buttons — but is dead. I clicked "Stage 1" twice, fully in view, no scroll ambiguity — both times `not actionable: not resolvable`. This is a real navigation regression: the one piece of in-page wayfinding that would let me jump straight to a gate's mechanism doesn't work, so I had to scroll manually through ~3000px of page.
- **`ibr scan` flagged 84 of 90 interactive-tagged elements as "looks interactive but has no click handler"** (rule `handler-integrity/fake-interactive`), including the FLOOR/BLUEPRINTS tabs, the Real/What-if toggle, the Gate Power switch, and every DEFAULT reset button. I want to flag this carefully: **most of those specific ones actually DID work when I clicked them for real** (FLOOR↔BLUEPRINTS switching, Intent Cascade toggle, ACQUIRE band popup, AUTO-FEED toggle, RUN THE DAY / PAUSE THE DAY all worked). So the scanner's static handler-detection is almost certainly a false-positive machine for whatever event-delegation pattern this page uses — but it did correctly co-locate with the truly dead set (reason tiles, counter tiles, Stage tabs). I can't fully separate "static analysis noise" from "real dead control" without clicking each one by hand, which I did for the ones listed above as confirmed-dead.
- **INSPECT A QUERY / INSPECT A PARTICLE**: not dead, but confusing on first click — if nothing happens to be mid-flight in the animation, the panel opens and says "No query is in flight right now. Run a query above, then try again while it is rising" (and the particle equivalent: "...while it is falling"). That's a legitimate transient-state message and I appreciated it once I understood it, but on first click it reads exactly like a dead button, indistinguishable in the moment from the reason-code tiles that really are dead. INSPECT A NODE and INSPECT AN EDGE worked immediately and paused the simulation with an explanation ("The simulation pauses while this stays open... Close it to resume").
- **"JUMP TO A JOB BY KEYBOARD" select** (the cron job dropdown next to the 24-hour dial): clicking it opened/focused the native select, but my attempt to programmatically choose "/api/cron/refresh-rss, every 15 minutes" reported the value as unreadable afterward — I couldn't verify the selection actually took. Might be a harness/native-select quirk rather than a real page bug, so I'm marking this uncertain rather than broken.
- **Nested glossary term "SSRF guard"** inside the expanded ACQUIRE mechanism popup, and the reason-code tiles under heavy auto-feed churn, both failed to resolve — but this looked like genuine DOM-replacement racing my click (the whole page re-renders roughly every second while auto-feed runs, evidenced by 250-300 elements added/removed per action even on unrelated clicks). Turning auto-feed off fixed churn-related failures for everything except the reason/counter tiles and Stage tabs, which stayed dead regardless.
- **"Gate enforcing a 0.50 floor... the run is paused" status line contradicted the live counters.** With auto-feed ON and the machine visualization scrolled out of view, the status message insists "the machine view is off screen or hidden, so the run is paused" — but the REJECTS counter kept climbing in the background the entire time (18 → 21 → 28 → 43 → 58 → 67 → 80 → 102 → 120 → 148 → 174 → 179 → 189 across successive screenshots while that "paused" message was showing). Either the pause logic doesn't actually stop the counters, or the copy is stale/wrong about what "paused" means. This is a real trust-eroding inconsistency: I was told nothing was happening while numbers visibly kept moving.

## 3. Real vs. synthetic — was the boundary clear, did I believe it

Yes, and this is the best-executed part of the whole page. Multiple independent signal layers agree with each other:
- Inline captions on every stat tile ("synthetic, no external baseline", "REJECT-CHUTE MANIFEST: REAL REASON CODES, SYNTHETIC COUNTS").
- A persistent corner label on the machine: "SCALE MODEL, SYNTHETIC COUNTS, REAL GATES."
- On BLUEPRINTS, a six-tier evidence taxonomy on every parameter: **MEASURED** (a number was actually recorded), **REASONED** (a written rationale exists in the repo), **INFERRED** (read from code, nobody stated it), **DEFAULT** (a bare constant nobody tuned), **BUILT NOT WIRED** (built and never switched on), **NO RECORD** (no rationale exists at all). That's a real epistemic-confidence system, not decoration — I saw it correctly applied (e.g., the 0.5 entity-confidence floor is tagged DEFAULT with the note "a bare constant with no comment and no sweep behind it... This checks only the sample environment; it is not a claim about production," cited to `sample.env.example, line ~895 · content-integrity.ts:46`).
- Explicit source citations (file path + line number) on every mechanism claim.
- The Maintenance Log actively documents where the repo lies to itself, rather than hiding it.

I believed it. Nothing on the page claimed something was live/production when it wasn't — if anything it undersells itself (calling a real, cited $1.27B/10%-parsing capital-flow rule "synthetic" would have been the easy lie; it doesn't). The one place the real/synthetic boundary got muddy was the "paused" bug in §2 — that's a functional bug, not a labeling-honesty problem.

## 4. Most interesting thing on the page — easy or hard to find

Tie between two things, and both required **zero clicks**, just scrolling:
1. The **Maintenance Log** (BLUEPRINTS §04, 9 self-contradictions) — genuinely hard to find. It's the last major section on BLUEPRINTS, roughly 3000px / 10 scroll-screens down, past the gates and index inventory, with no table of contents or working Stage-jump nav to shortcut to it (see §2). A skimmer would very plausibly never reach it.
2. The **Intent Cascade "best evidence not shipping" panel** (FLOOR §02) — easier to find, about 2 screens below the live machine, but its headline ("THIS IS THE BEST EVIDENCE ON THE PAGE, AND IT STILL IS NOT SHIPPING") undersells how good the actual A/B numbers are; you have to read the panel to get the 28/4/13 + 70.3% blast-radius numbers, they're not in any summary tile.

Both are prose you scroll to, not interaction you trigger — ironic, since the interactive stuff (reason tiles, counters) is exactly the part that's broken.

## 5. What I wanted to click that wasn't clickable

- Every reason-code tile, wanting the specific example fact/quote that got rejected under that code (e.g., what exact text tripped `off-vocabulary relation`). This is the single biggest missed opportunity — I have the aggregate count (e.g., "45 quote not grounded") but no way to see one.
- The counter tiles, wanting the same thing (e.g., click "FACTS KEPT" expecting a sample kept fact with its quote).
- Stage 1–5 tabs on BLUEPRINTS, wanting fast navigation instead of manual scrolling.
- The individual **MEASURED / DEFAULT / BUILT NOT WIRED / NO RECORD badges** themselves — I wanted to click one and get the underlying evidence file open directly, rather than reading it inline (which the page does provide inline anyway, so this is a nice-to-have, not a gap).

## 6. Where the page wasted my time

- **The server itself was unreachable at the start of the session** — `ERR_TIMED_OUT` / "This site can't be reached" on the very first load, and again on a mobile screenshot attempt mid-review. Root cause (visible from the repo): the original `serve.py` used a single-threaded `socketserver.TCPServer`, which serializes every request — with multiple review agents (presumably other personas in this same eval round) hitting the same 310KB page concurrently, requests queued behind each other and timed out. I had to `kill` the stuck process and restart it; mid-review, someone else patched `serve.py` to a `ThreadingTCPServer` and restarted it again, which is exactly the right fix, but it means the "frozen snapshot" wasn't reliably frozen-and-reachable for a chunk of the review. Not a page-design problem, but it's real wasted time and worth surfacing since it'll bite the next reviewer too if the threaded server isn't what ships.
- **Auto-feed churn racing my clicks.** With AUTO-FEED: ON, the whole tree re-renders (250-300 elements swapped) roughly every second, which repeatedly broke clicks on nested elements (glossary term "SSRF guard" inside an expanded mechanism popup, reason tiles) with `not actionable: not resolvable` errors that looked identical to genuinely dead controls. I had to explicitly toggle auto-feed off to tell the two apart. A curious human clicking around mid-simulation would hit the same false "is this broken?" moments.
- **Chasing the "paused" status message** (§2) cost real time — I re-verified it three separate times because the counters visibly contradicted the text, and I wanted to be sure I wasn't misreading a stale screenshot.

Desktop shows "dedupe, SHA-256(title,link,date-bucket), SSRF guard" in full; at 390px the same row only shows "dedupe" — the rest of the sentence is gone with no ellipsis, no wrap, and no horizontal-scroll affordance on that inner region. Same risk likely applies to the "grounding gate, no quote in block, dropped" and "query, top-k by cosine, fused with keywords" annotation lines in the TRANSFORM/RETRIEVE lanes, which were similarly truncated in my mobile capture.
- **390px mobile**: the sticky header logotype reads "THE FACT REFINERY" on first paint but drops to "FACT REFINERY" (loses "THE") after scrolling ~300px. Could be an intentional compact-header treatment, but nothing signals that it's deliberate — it reads as a glitch, not a design choice.
- Everything else I checked at both 1440px and 390px held up: no overlapping panels, no unreadable contrast, two-column PLANT CONTROLS / WHAT-IF LEVERS panels compress cleanly rather than break at mobile width, and the machine visualization (ACQUIRE/TRANSFORM/STORE/RETRIEVE/SURFACE) stays legible and non-overflowing at 390px.

## 8. The single change that would most improve it for me

**Make the reason-code tiles and counter tiles actually do something.** Everything the page does in prose (Maintenance Log, blast-radius panel, gate mechanism cards) is excellent and I trust it. But the entire *interactive* half of the dashboard — the part visually designed to invite clicking, styled identically to controls that do work — is dead. For a learner, the reject-reason tiles are the highest-value thing to click on the whole page: I want the one concrete example (article title + the exact quote that failed grounding, or the exact entity pair that failed a type constraint) behind "45 quote not grounded." Right now the page tells me the taxonomy and the count but never lets me see the artifact, which is exactly the kind of "trust me" gap the rest of the page goes out of its way to avoid.
