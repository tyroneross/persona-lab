Method assessment

Personas found the wall. They could not remember finding it.

Six personas reviewed an interactive technical page with no prior context. The method produced findings no checklist would have. Then five of six vanished before they could be asked a follow-up question.

Study: The Fact Refinery · 6 participants · 2026-08-21

Verdict

Keep the method. Move the memory out of the agent and into a file. The personas worked. The infrastructure holding them did not. A persona held in a running process has a memory measured in seconds, because a subagent becomes eviction-eligible thirty seconds after it finishes.

What worked

Blind reads found the failure the builders could not see

Three participants independently hit the same wall in the same place: the page opened with a metaphor before saying what the thing was. Nobody who had worked on it could see that, because everybody who had worked on it already knew.

"Cute, but where's the work?"Technical recruiter, first thirty seconds, on a phone

Role-specific goals produced role-specific findings

The value came from what each persona was trying to do, not from their job title. The recruiter was scanning on a phone between calls. The researcher was looking for a claim to attack. Those produced completely different findings from the same page.

Disagreement was the product

Two conflicts were worth more than any consensus. Experts called the glossary tooltips dead weight; novices depended on them. Three participants wanted a section deleted that three others named the best evidence on the page. Collapsing either into a panel view would have destroyed the finding and produced a worse page.

Asking for the exact phrase made findings actionable

Every persona was told to quote the words that lost them. That turned vague discomfort into a specific string a builder could locate and fix. The novice's own sentence, written when asked what would have fixed their confusion, became the page's opening line unedited.

What did not work

Persona memory lasted thirty seconds

Five of six follow-up interviews were lost. The transcripts still exist on disk; the ability to reach them does not. The single participant who happened to still be reachable then carried disproportionate weight in the analysis, which is its own bias.

Participants could not reply to the researcher

Messages arrived with no resolvable return address, so answers went to the orchestrator instead of the analyst. Two hops where there should have been one.

Participants are unreliable about causes

Two reported controls as broken. Three of four were not broken. They were gated, and every gate was silent. Reading the code found that. Asking the participants again would have confirmed the symptom and missed the cause entirely.

The method has no task-success or timing data

These are simulated participants giving reactions, not recruited users completing tasks under observation. That distinction bounds every claim the study can make, and it should be stated rather than implied.

A reported defect and a real defect are different objects. The study's most useful correction came from disbelieving a participant and checking the code.

The fix: the persona is the file, not the process

Identity already persisted. personas.json holds who a persona is. What was missing is what a persona has seen. A new encounter record adds it, written by the persona before it returns, so the return value is a pointer rather than the only copy.

FieldWhy it exists
verbatimAuthoritative and never summarised, so any later conclusion can be checked against what was actually said.

blindA blind read asks "does this work". An informed read asks "is this better". Mixing them silently invites a persona to rationalise a position it never held.
conditions.viewportsRequired. Half of this study's real defects existed only at phone width.

The UX researcher layer earned its place, for an unexpected reason

It was valuable as an adjudicator, not a summariser. Three things it did that a summary pass would not:

  • It read the code and found that three of four reported defects were silent gates rather than breakage.
  • It contradicted a participant who believed desktop had fixed a problem, correctly identifying that desktop had only moved a switch closer to the control it governed.
  • It declined a shortcut, refusing to reconstruct the five lost participants from their own prior reports, on the grounds that a persona handed its own answers will rationalise rather than react.

Keep the role, and give it the standing instruction that makes it work: trust your own observation over a participant's memory, and say plainly when a participant was mistaken. A researcher that only aggregates is a worse version of reading the transcripts yourself.

How to run the next one

  • Freeze the artifact and record its version. Builder edits racing participant sessions produce findings about a page that never existed.
  • Ask everything in the first dispatch. There is no reliable second turn.
  • Run blind by default. Compare rounds at the analyst level, where both are visible, rather than by showing a persona its own history.
  • Have each persona write its encounter before returning.
  • Verify every reported defect against the artifact before treating it as real.
